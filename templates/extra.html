What Is Pneumonia?

Pneumonia is an infection that affects the alveoli, which are tiny air sacs in your lungs. These air sacs are filled with air and help your body take in oxygen. When someone has pneumonia, the alveoli become inflamed and fill with fluid or pus. Because of this, the lungs can’t move oxygen.

Symptoms Of Pneumonia

Some symptoms of Pneumonia include:

  • Cough (may bring up mucus)
  • Fever or chills
  • Shortness of breath
  • Chest pain when breathing or coughing
  • Tiredness or weakness
  • Fast breathing or heartbeat
  • Loss of appetite
  • Nausea or vomiting

Causes of Pneumonia

Pneumonia can be caused by various microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, and fungi. The most common causes are:

  • Bacterial Pneumonia: Often caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae.
  • Viral Pneumonia: Caused by influenza viruses, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and coronaviruses.
  • Fungal Pneumonia: Caused by fungi like Histoplasma and Coccidioides.

Prevention and Treatment

Preventive measures include vaccination, good hygiene practices, and avoiding smoking. Some treatment options for pneumonia includes: antibiotics for bacterial pneumonia; antiviral medications for viral pneumonia; and antifungal treatments for fungal pneumonia. For more details, visit CDC Pneumonia Prevention.
